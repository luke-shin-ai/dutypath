# Benchmark: twelve goods, three agreements, three methods

Generated by `python scripts/benchmark.py --store store/dutypath-rules.rl` on the
committed snapshot. Every number here is printed by that command; none is an estimate.

## What is being measured

Twelve synthetic goods in chapters 39, 84 and 85, each with a bill of materials and a
hand written label for each of the three agreements, 36 labels in all. The labels were
read off the rule sentences in the documents, not copied from the engine: each one is
stored with the sentence it follows in `benchmark/gold.json`.

30 of the 36 labels turn on something a plain lookup does not read: an excluded interval,
a value ceiling, a value the bill of materials does not carry, a material in the same
heading, a second alternative, or an agreement with no rule for the line.

| Method | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| A, keyword | heading lookup by the first four digits of the rule's code, then a search of the rule text for heading, subheading or chapter |
| B, flat table | the same rows flattened to one key per code, string prefixes, first alternative only, no exclusion groups |
| C, DutyPath | interval matching over the graph, every alternative, the exclusion group, the value content, the weight condition, and a named gap instead of a guess |

## Results

| Method | Labels correct | Accuracy | Exception labels correct | Exception capture | Median time per market verdict |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A, keyword | 23 / 36 | 63.9% | 18 / 30 | 60.0% | 0.010 ms |
| B, flat table | 17 / 36 | 47.2% | 13 / 30 | 43.3% | 0.016 ms |
| C, DutyPath | 36 / 36 | 100.0% | 30 / 30 | 100.0% | 7.477 ms |

Zero leak, measured on the store the run used: sha256 before `200d613c08a4c4c6`, after `200d613c08a4c4c6`, unchanged `True`.
No method wrote a shipment row: A and B read a JSON export of the rules and never open the
store, and C opens it read only and proves it with the hash and the label counts. The
numbers say the same thing in three different ways, and the third way is checkable by a
reader who only has the file.

## What the engine still cannot answer

6 of the 36 labels are UNDECIDABLE, and each of them is a place where the snapshot,
rather than the engine, is short:

* **No rule for the line.** RCEP Annex 3A as extracted covers 108 headings in the three
  chapters; 85.07, 84.81, 85.13 and 85.09 are not among them, so the verdict names the
  missing rule instead of guessing one.
* **A specific or compound duty.** A schedule cell such as "22.40 yen per kilogram or 6.5
  percent, whichever is the less" is printed in the snapshot and never turned into a number:
  the engine says it does not compute it.
* **A line the schedule excludes for Korea.** Japan's schedule prints `U` on some lines for
  Korean goods, for example 3901.10.020; the verdict reports the exclusion and prices the
  shipment at MFN, which is the right answer and a different one from "no rate".
* **A rule that needs process data.** The EU's alternative for integrated circuits is
  diffusion, which a bill of materials does not describe; only the heading change and the
  value ceiling of that row are read.

## What building this harness changed

The first run of this comparison disagreed with the hand labels on six labels, and every
one of the six was an engine defect rather than a label error:

* a rule printing both a build-up and a build-down threshold was checked as though the good
  had to meet both, when the sentence offers them as alternatives;
* an alternative that names a permitted source subheading ("from subheading 8513.90,
  whether or not there is also a change from any other heading") was read as a plain heading
  change, so a good the agreement accepts was refused.

Both are fixed, and both now have a test. A benchmark that only ever agrees with the engine
measures nothing; this one disagreed first.

## What this does not measure

Twelve goods chosen to exercise the rules are not a random sample, so the accuracy figures
describe these rules, not the whole nomenclature. The snapshot covers three chapters, and
a label is only as good as the sentence it cites. What the table does establish is that the
engine reads the parts of a rule that a lookup does not, and that the difference shows up on
the labels that decide money: the excluded interval, the value ceiling and the missing value.
